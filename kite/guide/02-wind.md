# Wind

Wind decides everything about a kite day, so learn to read it. Steady wind of roughly 8 to 25 km/h suits most single-line and sport kites. Lighter air needs a light kite and plenty of patience. Stronger wind makes pulling heavy and raises the risk of broken lines and injuries.

Watch flags, leaves, grass and ripples on water. Smooth, even air is best. Gusty, turbulent wind near hills, buildings and trees makes a kite dive and surge, so move to open ground when you can.

Face away from the wind to feel its direction on your neck. The area in front of you, where the kite can fly, is called the wind window. It is a quarter-sphere downwind from you, and the kite pulls hardest near its centre and least near its edges.

Check the forecast before leaving home. If a storm, thunder or sudden gusts approach, pack up straight away.
