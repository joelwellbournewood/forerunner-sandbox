# Lines

Lines are the only link between you and the kite, so treat them with care. Choose line rated well above the pull you expect. Thin line suits light wind, while thicker line is stronger and better in strong wind.

Before every flight, run each line through your fingers and look for nicks, frays, kinks and knots. Replace anything damaged, because a weak spot will fail at the worst moment. On a two- or four-line kite, check that the lines are matched in length, as uneven lines make the kite turn on its own.

Never wrap line around your hands or wrists. Use handles or a winder, and wear gloves in strong wind, since running line can burn skin quickly.

Wind line onto the winder in even, tidy turns, without twisting. Keep lines dry and out of long sun. Do not let anyone walk across stretched lines, and never fly near people who could walk into them.
